# Section 2 — Getting Started

_Status: headings drafted; prose to come._

A hands-on introduction. After a short setup, a sequence of small tutorials builds a complete scene one idea at a time — each ends with something you can hear. Working through them in order is the fastest way to learn GX2; the later sections explain in depth what the tutorials touch on.

## Before you start — what you need

What it takes to run GX2 (where it runs, sound, and optional MIDI gear), so you are ready before the first tutorial.

## First launch

Opening GX2 for the first time: the starter scene, the parts of the window at a glance, and getting the audio engine running (the first-gesture / Load Engine step).

## Tutorial 1 — Your first sound

Open a starter scene, press play, and hear it. Stop, rewind, and change the tempo. You learn the transport and the idea that a scene makes sound on its own.

## Tutorial 2 — A curve with a beat

Add a curve and place a few beat points, then watch the cursor sweep along it and fire them. You learn objects, beat points, and the cursor.

## Tutorial 3 — Let colour choose the notes

Drop in a background image and map the colour under each beat to pitch. You learn the image as a musical signal.

## Tutorial 4 — A sprite on the move

Add a sprite, give it some motion, and watch it drift across the image and bounce off the edges; make it sound where it goes. You learn motion and a first taste of collisions.

## Tutorial 5 — Your first script

Open the Script tab and write a short callback that plays a note from a value in the scene. You learn the authoring surface and the sound API.

## Tutorial 6 — Change it while it plays

Edit the script during playback and hear the change immediately. You learn the live-coding side of GX2 — that the scene and its script are both live.

## Where to go next

Pointers from each tutorial into the deeper sections (Curves, Beat Points, Colour, Motion, Scripting), so a reader can follow whichever thread caught their interest.
